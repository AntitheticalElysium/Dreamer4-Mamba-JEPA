# Mechanism audit, 2026-09-26

This is a post-hoc mechanism audit on previously inspected roots and immutable checkpoints. It does **not** certify a new gate or a causal treatment effect of W's training recipe. New read-only interventions and corrected analyses are in this directory.

## 1. Why CLS/z misses a nearby zombie

The largest measured failure is **loss of location during global aggregation**. On 300 paired Craftax states, replacing one empty grass tile with a zombie next to the player versus far away changes the trained CLS in almost the same direction: mean cosine 0.99986, pairwise cosine 0.99972; the near/far contrast is 0.00077 of a natural CLS radius. At initialization mean cosine was already 0.99988. The matching right-neighbour patch token gives near/far Fisher d-prime 4.35 at step 10k. The final CLS and z give 0.031 and 0.018. A nearby zombie's day Fisher d-prime in z falls from 1.79 at step 0 to 0.51 at 10k; at night 0.99 to 0.28, while the corresponding patch token rises 4.24 to 4.36 (day) and 5.02 to 9.55 (night). This is a **controlled input edit**; the rest of each game state is identical. Sources: `../20260926_diagnosis/twins.py`, `../20260926_diagnosis/twins.json`, `twins_location.py/json`.

A separate same-root FIT/54k ridge probe (2,408 FIT, 1,205 judgement, 117 judgement episodes) reads zombie adjacency from random-initialized CLS at AUC 0.663, final CLS 0.610, final z 0.594, and the local mean of four neighbouring patch tokens 0.999. The trained-minus-initial CLS difference is -0.053, episode bootstrap 95% [-0.092,-0.015]. A global mean of all patch tokens is only 0.628. Lava adjacency from final CLS is 0.963. `cls_layers.py/json`.

The spatial failure extends to terrain. A paired near/far lava edit gives trained CLS response cosine 0.9987 and a near/far Fisher d-prime 0.099 (versus 19.15 in the local token); water gives cosine 0.9997 and d-prime 0.234 (versus 27.75 local). On the very same natural 54k roots, global lava **count alone** predicts lava adjacency at AUC 0.964, matching CLS 0.963. Global zombie count reaches only 0.728 for zombie adjacency, while trained CLS is 0.610. Thus the apparent CLS lava advantage can be explained by a scene-level proxy and is not proof it preserved lava location. `terrain_location.py/json`, `global_proxy.py/json`.

The positional embedding norm is only 0.285 versus 3.58 for the patch-content embedding at the final checkpoint (8%); it barely changed during training. Multiplying **only** the frozen patch-position embeddings by 8 raises the day near/far CLS contrast d-prime from 0.031 to 0.221, but leaves it weak. Zeroing positions reduces the contrast to 0.003. This verifies that location enters mainly through this weak positional channel, but does not prove that re-training with larger positions would solve it. `pos_scale.py/json`.

Thus the slogan “JEPA suppresses random zombies” is incomplete. The failure exists at step 0 before JEPA can suppress anything; training worsens it. Littwin et al.'s theorem is for a deep *linear* model under restricted covariance assumptions, not a proof about this Craftax ViT. The controlled data support weak location encoding at the CLS interface for both mobs and terrain, followed by additional training suppression of the small mob signal; they do not isolate the training objective from every other training effect.

## 2. What the transition does and does not preserve

The previous phrase “transitions universally degrade” is false: whole-state safe-choice retention varies by block. The sharper failure is **action-specific physical consequence**. On previously opened 55k-58k blocks, real successor U lets its own head choose safely on 0.975-0.981 of zombie opportunity roots; generated U only 0.490-0.546. W is 0.990-0.999 real versus 0.541-0.636 generated (`../20260926_diagnosis/choices.json`).

A held-out **linear** diagnostic (fit 55k-56k, test 57k-58k) reads *damage* from true successor U at AUC 0.989, Mamba output `h` 0.754, and generated latent U 0.648 (`transition_corrected.py/json`). **Correction:** that apparent `h`→projector drop is probe-dependent. Equal-capacity nonlinear within-root ranking probes on the same split give AUC 0.893 for root U plus action, 0.895 for `h`, and 0.886 for generated U, across three seeds (`damage_rank_h_vs_g.py/json`). Neither a projector-specific information loss nor a world-generated advantage over root plus action is established. The world’s physical predictions can still be wrong even when a supervised probe extracts a useful action prior from its states. The prior movement test incorrectly counted lava entry as blocked (7-11% of its blocked label). We preserved the original report and reran with lava excluded: traversability AUC for patch tokens 0.985, root U 0.697, `h` 0.695, generated U 0.666, true successor U 0.808; W generated 0.676. The generated-minus-root transition loses some of the already weakened local navigation signal. Its move-size statistic is near chance (~0.49) versus ~0.92 for real successor, suggesting generated movement does not follow the physical scroll reliably; do not read the statistic as an oracle information ceiling.

The older U spectral diagnostic found 94% of its real fatal-direction weight in PCA components carrying 5% of action-effect energy; top-10 component effect R² 0.83 and ranks 100-192 0.23. But a closed-form ridge fit raises tail R² to 0.42 without fixing fatal alignment (generated AUC 0.596); 3x training and W did not fix it either. Therefore “the optimizer simply learns the tail last” is **refuted as a sufficient explanation**. Low target weight, factual counterfactual coverage, target stochasticity and projector mapping remain distinguishable candidates. A simple visible-state rule based on passable adjacent tiles and zombie positions scores 0.977-0.982 expected safety on zombie roots across 55k-58k (`../20260926_diagnosis/decision.json`), so one-step action selection itself is not prevented by hidden randomness.

## 3. Why the canonical H2 head failed

The terminal sampler tail-aligns each episode. `bridge_rollout(... depth=2)` fixes death at the second generated step, and `paired_terminal_loss` reads exactly the last alive/dead pair. Hence generation depth predicts the label. A prior fixed-position replay found P(dead) 0.73 at generated death depth 2 and 0.56 even 10 frames before; at evaluator depth 1 it is about 0.05 at either time, with death discrimination AUC ~0.51. The real death frame can be read (AUC ~0.84). This is a demonstrated **label alias**, not a hypothesized property of JEPA. It is still present on main; `h2-terminal-depth` fixes the layout but is not merged or validated as a standalone causal repair.

There is a second decision-head effect: argmin across 17 scores often picks an outlier among 13 stay actions. H2's mean predicted death risk for staying exceeds moving by 0.086-0.105 on zombie roots, yet its selected action is a stay on 74-79% and its zombie safety is only 0.19-0.26 (`../20260926_diagnosis/choices.json`). Mean category separation is insufficient: on 76-81% of these roots, at least one stay action has lower predicted death risk than every safe move. The minimum score over many stay actions drives choice.

## 4. What W demonstrably improves

W is an invertible fixed rescaling of U, so **its input contains exactly the same information**. But W's training is not a geometry-only intervention. In U coordinates, the old per-component loss weights span 48.1x; W's target rescaling plus approximately uniform W-coordinate weights makes effective coefficients span **2315.6x**. It also changes world activations, optimization trajectory, agent-readout input and co-trained heads. `whiten.py`, `interface.py`, saved `interface_pool_v1/pool.pt`.

W's native head beats U in safe choice, but evidence does **not** show substantially improved fatal-state fidelity: W and U generated fatal-direction AUC 0.593 vs 0.592 on 58k; their real-fitted generated death AUC 0.650 vs 0.655 on 56k. W's tail effect R² is 0.21 against U's 0.30. `../20260921_readout_ladder/evidence/whiten.json`, `whiten_fidelity.json`.

The per-action decomposition is more specific. W changes the *choice rule*: on zombie roots across 55k-58k, it chooses a stay only 2-4% of the time, versus U's 18-24%, while successful move choices rise from 41-50% to 51-61%. On 58k W-U safe choice is +0.127 [0.082,0.176]; **+0.077 [0.059,0.096]** is reproduced by their state-ignored action mixtures and +0.051 [0.007,0.096] remains from root-dependent selection. On 55k-57k the state-dependent remainder is unresolved, although the action-mix component is positive and resolved. Cross-fitting action mixtures on the other three blocks reproduces W-U gains of +0.064 to +0.074. `choice_decomp.py/json`, saved per-action dump. W is therefore substantially a better movement-biased head, not an established repair of simulated damage. W still chooses unsafe-or-blocked movement 36-47% of the time, and its generated damage AUC is only 0.662.

The `headwhite.py` arm cannot attribute “half to head, half to world”: its phase 2 updates the world together with the head. LeJEPA's isotropy theorem motivates an intervention but cannot infer why this W run improved. Moreover canonical z is not isotropic empirically (6,220x covariance eigenvalue spread), despite SIGReg.

## Mechanism verdict

**Identified:** (a) CLS loses *where* the zombie is, largely from initialization; (b) its training further suppresses the nearby-zombie signal; (c) generated states fail to reproduce physical movement/damage with true-successor fidelity, while a projector-specific information loss is not supported by the nonlinear probe; (d) H2 continuation supervision aliases death to generation depth; (e) W's observed safe-choice gain is largely a shift away from stay actions, with a smaller state-specific component on one block.

**Not identified:** the causal parameter inside training that makes W shift its choice rule; whether the weak consequence in generated U/W is dominated by target noise, inadequate factual action support, the MSE weighting, or a specific world/projector limitation. The existing W/U comparison changes all of those training coordinates at once. Claiming that “isotropy fixes imagined consequence” or that “JEPA randomness explains CLS” goes beyond the evidence.

## 5. New causal localization and correction to the transition account

`cls_depth.py/json` corrected a hidden-state index offset (`cls_depth_offset_bug.json` retains the invalid first pass). Across 300 paired states, the controlled near-versus-far zombie contrast is already nearly absent in CLS after the **first** attention block: d-prime 0.047 at initialization and 0.063 after training, versus 5.62 and 5.98 in the local patch token. At the final block it is 0.050 at initialization and 0.034 after training, versus 6.23 in the trained local token. Trained CLS near/far response cosine rises from 0.99905 after block 1 to 0.99986 at the end. The final projection is not where location disappears. Global aggregation has minimal position sensitivity from its first block; training attenuates the remaining contrast. The weak frozen positional embedding and the position-rescaling test above support that mechanism, though they do not isolate the JEPA objective's causal contribution.

`pool_vs_pca.py/json` splits the next interface loss into two stages on held-out 55k+56k fit / 57k+58k test roots. For each of the four adjacent move directions, the same standardized ridge readout predicts visible passability with mean AUC **0.985** from 81 patch tokens, **0.820** from the 4×4 pooled grid, and **0.697** from the 192-dimensional PCA U state. Lava was excluded from the blocked label: entering lava is a *successful but fatal* move. Although PCA retains 99% of total patch-grid variance, it loses much of this local, low-energy decision cue. A 256-wide nonlinear MLP on U reaches only mean AUC **0.722** (`passability_nonlinear.py/json`); this is an operational readout deficit, not an information-theoretic proof of erasure.

The nonlinear damage-probe correction above is important. The final projector cannot be singled out for the native head's failure. A probe trained with action outcomes extracts **0.893** within-root damage AUC from root U plus action, **0.895** from Mamba h, and **0.886** from the projected generated U. Hence all three largely predict damage through the context and action under that probe, while the generated state still differs materially from a real successor. The real successor makes damage AUC near 0.99 and the native head chooses safely on ~0.98 of zombie roots; generated U's native head is ~0.49-0.55. This is failure of *fidelity and trained-head use*, not proof of absent mutual information.

## 6. The SLEEP shortcut is a simulator-and-data mechanism, not just an action prior

Craftax Classic applies the action and movement, then zombie attacks, then player intrinsics (`craftax_classic/game_logic.py:1655-1686`). A hit to a player *already sleeping* does 7 damage and wakes them before rendering (`:830-845`). Pressing SLEEP in the same step as a hit instead receives the awake 2 damage, then starts sleeping in `update_player_intrinsics` (`:1237-1243`). Thus a fatal SLEEP successor can be rendered *asleep*, the rare counterexample to the corpus's “asleep-looking means alive” shortcut. The frozen renderer detector was validated with no errors on 90,656 labelled frames (`../20260926_diagnosis/sleep.py`).

On the exact 32,647-window U/W training pool, dead frames are asleep only **253/8,155 = 3.1%**, while **44,018/187,727 = 23.4%** of alive frames are asleep (`exact_pool_sleep.py/json`). P(dead | asleep frame) is 0.57%, versus 5.21% for awake frames. In the 8,071 terminal-window last alive→dead pairs, **4,147** go asleep→awake-dead and only **167** go awake→asleep-dead; all 167 are SLEEP actions (`terminal_sleep_pairs.py/json`). This is strong evidence for the shortcut and shows that its counterexample is rare, rather than wholly absent.

On 1,971 already-opened zombie opportunity roots across 55k-58k, actual P(death | SLEEP) is **0.83-0.89** but U's native head reports only **0.25-0.27** and W's **0.30-0.33**. SLEEP is chosen on 17-23% of U roots and 2-4% of W roots. Forbidding only SLEEP in U's saved action scores raises its expected safe choice from **0.516 to 0.579**, recovering **0.063** of W's **0.082** advantage (~78%); paired episode-bootstrap intervals are [0.049, 0.078] and [0.059, 0.104] respectively (`sleep_score_intervention.py/json`). This is a *post-hoc behavioral intervention*, not proof of what parameter in W's training caused the shift. W remains poorly calibrated on SLEEP and still picks blocked moves on many roots. On roots where U selects SLEEP and W another action, W both raises the SLEEP score and lowers its best alternative score (`score_margin.py/json`); no single logit explains the entire choice shift.

A frozen-U-world, three-seed head-only scaling control (`../20260926_diagnosis/headonly.py/json`) improves true-SLEEP death AUC from 0.920-0.925 to 0.933-0.938, but predicted fatal probability remains 0.22-0.30 versus true 0.85. This shows an actual, modest head-conditioning effect without establishing it as W's full causal mechanism.

## 7. Stochasticity, objective geometry and long-horizon fidelity

`mean_bound.py/json` uses 1,002 full simulator roots from 900k+, each forked under all 17 actions and four random keys. In common unwhitened U units, root/real-successor encodings of U and W agree to 1.9e-6. Key variation contributes only **0.00073** of normalized one-step successor variance; U's one-step error is **0.0457**, W's **0.1176**, and simply copying the root is **0.1056**. On the factual logged action alone the errors are U **0.0359**, W **0.0954**, copy **0.0604**. W is a *worse physical predictor in a common state space*, even while its native head selects more safe actions. Full-state repeated keys measure stochasticity conditional on the full simulator state; they do not measure uncertainty from hidden state given pixels. Yet visible passability is deterministic, so hidden randomness cannot explain the large blocked-versus-moved error.

`error_spectrum.py/json` explains the optimization tradeoff concretely. The first ten PCA coordinates contain **70.7%** of true one-step action-effect energy. Historical U's weighted loss gives them **25.7%** of effect-weighted loss; W's rescaled loss gives them **3.2%**. U predicts their effects with R² **0.671**, W with R² **-0.127**. The bottom 92 coordinates receive 22.2% versus 54.6% and improve from R² **-0.209** to **-0.075** under W, still below copying. W thus reallocates error toward low-variance coordinates at the expense of major physical effects. This measured tradeoff does not by itself identify whether the head gain comes from loss weighting, coordinate conditioning, or both: W changes all three.

On the same BC-trajectories, recursive rollout error in common U units grows from **0.035 to 1.21** over 16 steps for U and **0.093 to 3.04** for W, while teacher-forced one-step error at each depth stays near 0.04 and 0.09-0.15 respectively (`../20260926_diagnosis/compound.py/json`). Key noise at depth 16 is about **0.026**, much smaller than model error. The recursive distribution shift is the observed mechanism of compounding, not a claim that key stochasticity never matters at other horizons or roots.

The frozen-world output-projector refit (`projector_refit.py/json`) also undercuts a simple “insufficient projector training” explanation: linear factual-only and all-action refits yield one-step MSE **0.105** and **0.062**, worse than the saved nonlinear projector's **0.044** on the same split. A different *nonlinear* decoder might improve specific control targets; this refit only rejects a low-capacity linear shortcut under its MSE target.

The crossed 2×2 coordinate/loss retrain is in `whitening_factorial.py`, with native-head comparison in `whitening_factorial_score.py`. It holds the pool, architecture, init seeds, sampler, updates and terminal layout fixed; the other two cells distinguish raw-U coordinates with W's effective loss metric from whitened-W coordinates with U's effective metric. Its result must be read as exploratory on already-inspected 55k-58k roots, with one world seed per arm.

## 2026-09-28 notebook audit and generated-fit correction

The newer NOTEBOOK.md contains a causal intervention against treating canonical CLS as a hard architectural ceiling: paired Raw+LDAD(10) training changes root-z zombie-adjacency AUC **0.606 -> 0.961**, four-direction passability **0.588 -> 0.855**, and health R² **0.122 -> 0.728** (../20260927_levers/ldad_eval.json). Its true-token-to-imagined readouts improve at one step but fade with depth, and factual prediction MSE grows about 5.3x. Thus canonical Raw CLS's location loss is measured; its inevitability is refuted. The training objective can make CLS retain action-sensitive facts, but this LDAD recipe is not yet a useful rollout repair.

The historical levers teval reports fitted ridge fact probes on **true** TRAIN-seed tokens and applied them to imagined TEST-seed tokens. The corrected `teval.py` now writes schema-v2 reports with both true-fit and generated-fit readouts; the original reports remain unchanged. We ran a matched control on the 18k corrt world (generated_fit.py/json): same ridge capacity, visible labels, split and validation rule, changing only the probe's training inputs from true to that world's generated states. On 281 alive held-out roots at depth 16, adjacent-zombie AUC rises **0.530 -> 0.606**, difference **+0.076** with 95% walk-seed-cluster bootstrap **[+0.015,+0.141]** across 43 seeds. At depth 8 it rises **0.581 -> 0.650**; at depth 16 all-cell zombie AUC rises **0.499 -> 0.651**, health R² **0.590 -> 0.641**. The transfer penalty is real, and the notebook's unqualified depth-16 fact scores understate what is linearly readable from generated states. The generated-fit adjacent-zombie score remains below the multi-key full-simulator benchmark **0.802** (../20260927_levers/ceiling.json). This comparison is not an information-theoretic ceiling or an actor result: the generated-fit probe can extract context priors, and the true future is partly stochastic. Token-space error and ground-truth substitution results do not depend on this readout transfer.

The newer context tests identify an independent canonical implementation defect: the Mamba is trained on four-frame windows but deployed with unbounded recurrence. With identical joint weights, 16-step imagined error / natural variance changes **2.57 -> 0.53** when deployment truncates to the source LeWM three-frame history (../20260926_diagnosis/sliding.json). Long-context training or bounded inference is therefore required for a valid canonical rollout. The branch-only terminal-depth alias fix does not repair this context mismatch.

The interrupted Stage-2 fmamba arm was evaluated and preserved at ../20260927_levers/evals/corrt_raw_suffix_s7_fmamba.json. Relative to full corrt at 6k, one-step error / copy improves **0.302 -> 0.245** and depth-16 error / variance **0.810 -> 0.745** (paired seed-cluster intervals exclude zero), while blocked moves worsen **0.913 -> 0.978**. Canvas has now finished: against fmamba6k it changes depth-16 error **0.745 -> 0.739**, an unresolved difference **-0.0058 [-0.0187,+0.0083]**; it improves blocked-move error **0.978 -> 0.919** but slightly worsens moved error **0.191 -> 0.195**. The full model's 18k snapshot is better (**0.173**, **0.683**) than canvas6k (**0.243**, **0.739**) and took less total training time (3,451 versus 4,984 seconds). That does not reject factorization as a class; it rejects these runs as a reason to replace the full model now. Factored attention and raster scan have since completed; see NOTEBOOK.md Stage 2 and the new result below.

For the same 18k checkpoint and held-out roots, depth-16 token error / natural variance is **0.683** recursively imagined versus **0.065** teacher-forced and **1.071** for copying the root (../20260927_levers/evals/corrt_raw_suffix_s7_u18000.json). The model predicts much better when conditioned on real preceding frames, so compounding rollout drift is separately measured even after the generated-fit readout correction.

## 2026-09-29 canvas causal checks and LDAD readout rescore

`../20260927_levers/canvas_diag.json` measures the scroll estimator at 99.3% agreement on the first imagined step, falling to 76.4% at steps 9-16.
