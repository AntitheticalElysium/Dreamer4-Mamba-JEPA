# Motion-aligned Mamba carry: mechanism audit and decision rule

The proposed operation has a concrete target. In `fmamba`, six spatial-attention layers each send the 82 fixed screen positions through separate temporal Mamba-2 streams. After a successful Craftax move, a terrain cell changes screen position, while its former stream retains both the convolution history and the FP32 SSM state. The proposed variant reindexes **both** carries before it processes the new frame. Action and HUD streams remain screen-fixed; unmatched map cells get zero carries, matching Mamba’s normal initial state. On the same **16,028** alive factual transitions, **5,690** high-confidence scrolls reveal **46,126** border cells: **12.87%** of map slots on a scrolled frame, or **4.57%** of all alive map-time slots. A bounded view must therefore initialize these frequently; retaining off-screen identities, as `fcanvas` does, is a different long-memory policy. In symbols, for a matched map cell `j <- i`, `(conv, ssm)_{t,j}^{before} = (conv, ssm)_{t-1,i}^{after}`. The prototype is in `carry_transport.py` and `transport_world.py`.

```text
fixed fmamba:       terrain P at screen i -> carry[i] ; after scroll P at j -> carry[j]
transported fmamba: terrain P at screen i -> carry[i] ; after scroll P at j -> carry[i] moved to j
```

This does **not** follow automatically from `corrg`. Its scalar action-token output raises or lowers the four neighbor-copy logits; it does not estimate a pixel displacement field and currently has moved-versus-blocked AUC **0.778** on the prior factual panel (`../20260927_levers/corrg_diag.json`). The action supplies the *direction* of a successful move, but not whether the move succeeded. The prototype instead uses the already tested token-pair shift estimator. Its real one-step agreement with the simulator is **99.35%** overall and **97.52%** on moved actions (`../20260927_levers/scroll.json`). Its factual move-attempt agreement is **98.23%** on **4,008** root-action forks, with **100%** on **1,147** blocked moves and **97.52%** on **2,861** successful moves (`scroll_hazard.json`). Near a visible zombie, agreement is **97.62%** on **336** move attempts, including **96.08%** on **204** successful moves; those counts are small. Its accuracy degrades under generated frames, so a gain using true inputs would not establish deployable long-horizon gain.

There is a better *learned* move cue in the prior `corrt` output head: on **1,132** held-out move attempts, its target-tile decision logit reached moved/blocked AUC **0.99756** (`../20260927_levers/probe_corrt_raw_suffix_s7.json`), versus **0.79304** for the shared `corrg` frame logit (`../20260927_levers/corrg_probe.json`). This is not yet a thresholded accuracy or generated-frame reliability measurement. Critically, `corrt` reads the final-layer target feature at frame `t`; that can causally decide where to shift carries for `t+1`, but it is unavailable to the current implementation, which scans all times through layer 1 before computing final-layer frame `t`. A learned-gate version needs frame-outer/layer-inner execution or a separate early predictor, and a matched `corrt` no-transport control. The existing prototype deliberately tests the simpler state-pair estimator; it is not a claim to have reused `corrt` correspondence.

The new factual diagnostic (`registration_scope.json`, prior inspected diagnosis panel) used an independent visible-terrain rule, calibrated on the stored one-step simulator labels. It classified **2,750/2,861** moved and **1,138/1,147** blocked root-action pairs and abstained on the rest; every classified pair matched the simulator. Among **16,028** alive factual future transitions, it identified **5,690** high-confidence scrolls, **2,245** blocks and **400** ambiguous move attempts. On overlapping map cells in those scrolls, same-screen-slot patch-token MSE was **0.27066**; corresponding-terrain MSE was **0.04092**, a **84.88%** reduction [**83.66%, 85.96%**] by seed-cluster bootstrap. This quantifies the *input identity mismatch*, not a model prediction improvement. Zombie occupancy changed on **1,936/312,344 = 0.62%** of overlapping cells; those transitions have much larger aligned-token error, so a single global scroll cannot follow both terrain and independent mob motion.

The player-local follow-up (`registration_hazard.json`, same inspected factual panel) narrows that statement. Across **5,690** high-confidence scrolls, the central **3×3** map patch-token MSE falls from **0.27363** to **0.13041** after terrain alignment: a **52.34%** reduction [**48.93%, 55.45%**] by seed cluster. It is much smaller than the **84.88%** full-map reduction because the center includes the player and changing local content. Visible zombie occupancy changes in **813/51,210 = 1.59%** of those local cells and on **544/5,690 = 9.56%** of scroll steps. A global terrain shift is therefore incomplete precisely near the player; the local residual cannot be attributed solely to zombies because player overlays and other content also change.

The more relevant frozen-layer check (`mixer_registration.json`) measures the **actual first temporal-Mamba input** after positional embeddings, spatial attention and RMS normalization, with the exact historical Raw `corrt/fmamba` checkpoint source verified. On **704** high-confidence factual scroll pairs from **139** seeds, terrain alignment lowers that tensor’s overlapping-map MSE from **0.25180** to **0.08364**, a **66.78%** reduction [**64.76%, 68.70%**] by seed cluster. For the central 3×3 around the player it lowers **0.25312** to **0.17576**, only **30.56%** [**25.59%, 34.92%**]. The raw patch input on those exact pairs improves **86.01%** full-map and **53.26%** central. Thus the alignment mismatch survives into Mamba’s input, but attention/position/action and changing local content shrink it where safety matters. This is still a feature-registration diagnostic, not a forecast or action gain.

A four-stage decomposition on those same **704** pairs (`positional_registration.json`) explains why the raw-token alignment gain shrinks before recurrence. Relative MSE reduction is **85.19%** after the learned input projection, **82.12%** after adding screen position, **81.21%** after time position, and **66.78%** after spatial attention/normalization. These are paired factual feature distances, not a causal ablation of trained performance. Screen-position contamination accounts for only about **3.1 percentage points** of the reduction drop; the largest measured change occurs across the spatial-attention/normalization stage (**14.4 points**), whose output mixes current scene context into each stream. Thus simply transporting a carry is not equivalent to tracking pure terrain identity.

The code mechanism test (`carry_transport_smoke.json`) passes exact index/border/HUD mapping and gradient checks. With no shift, the Mamba-2 output differs from a single uninterrupted scan by at most **1.19e-7**, and the complete spatial-attention/Mamba layer by **2.38e-7**. Mamba's carry costs **68,608 bytes per token per layer** at the bf16 convolution/FP32 SSM training dtype, or **33.76 MB per sample** over 82 streams and six layers *before activations*. The measured GPU cost appears below.

The nearest local predecessor is `fcanvas`, which already scans tokens in world-aligned canvas cells. Its result limits the novelty claim: on the previous six-frame experiment, its overall one-step and depth-16 gains over fixed-slot Mamba were small or unresolved (`../20260927_levers/canvas_context.json`). It is not an exact carry-transport test because unseen canvas cells continued through Mamba's convolution despite SSM `dt=0` (`../20260927_levers/canvas_diag.json`), and it retains off-screen canvas cells while the bounded-screen prototype discards them. The prior frozen-world correction that held **both** convolution and SSM state on unseen canvas cells already changed depth-16 error /V by only **-0.000088**, seed CI **[-0.000877,+0.000521]** (`../20260927_levers/canvas_hold_oracle.json`); therefore the known convolution-hold defect did not explain its rollout loss. On windows without reentry, the completed frozen-weight comparison confirms numerical near-equivalence, using 15 each from no-scroll, scroll without reentry and scroll with reentry (input-only stratification over 1,002 existing roots). Historical `tworld.py` source hash was reproduced exactly by removing only the later `ldad1` pool registration, and the comparator checks that reconstructed hash against the checkpoint. Prior fcanvas oracle shift substitution improved depth-16 error /V by only **0.01055** [difference CI **-0.01697, -0.00498**] (`../20260927_levers/canvas_oracle.json`), so shift-estimator errors alone do not explain the full rollout loss.

The source literature supports the *class of mechanism*, not this result. [TrajGRU](https://arxiv.org/html/1706.03458v2) learns spatially varying recurrent links; [gated recurrent flow propagation](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.08871v2) warps a recurrent segmentation state and gates uncertain alignment; [Warp LSTM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03922) propagates spatiotemporal memory using a warp; [BasicVSR++](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.13371v1) flow-aligns propagated features and discusses occlusion; [MADiff](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02638v2) changes Mamba's selective scan using egomotion; [Yang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05997v2) measure spatial-registration failures in ordinary RNN/SSM memory; [MGMVFI](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22861v1) moves/reorders Mamba inputs along motion and handles bad correspondence. None transports both Mamba-2 carry tensors in this Craftax architecture, nor establishes a control gain here.

## Predeclared decision sequence and disposition

1. Frozen same-weight comparison: **completed**; carry transport and `fcanvas` agree numerically on no-reentry windows.
2. Matched GPU resource check: **completed**; warmed batch-40 transport is 4.92x slower, though it fits the 6 GB GPU. The first cold-start-contaminated timing is retained separately.
3. Raw-only six-frame transport retrain: **not launched**, because the distinct-mechanism condition did not hold. It would mostly replicate the existing `fcanvas` experiment.
4. A future long-context/reentry treatment requires a separately declared matched control and a measured cross-scroll memory deficit from the ongoing integration campaign. If that treatment fails, compare true, generated, no and wrong shifts on identical prefixes, then inspect carry norms, border/reentry error and decision readout.

A global shift registers terrain, not independently moving mobs. If the eventual deficit is concentrated on zombies whose location changes relative to terrain, the next mechanism needs local flow, object slots, or a separate dynamic stream. Mixing current `corrg` soft-copy weights into FP32 SSM carries is a different intervention: it can blur multiple histories and introduces a whole-model temporal dependency. It should be tested only after the hard global-shift baseline identifies a relevant residual.

A direct learned-cue diagnostic ran after the resource test (`learned_gate_rollout.py`, `lane8_learned_gate.sh`). It fits only a threshold on TRAIN-seed true one-step `corrt` logits; then it scores held-out true one-step moves and the same frozen fcanvas `corrt` world on its own generated prefixes at depths 1, 2–4, 5–8, 9–16. The simulator-visible terrain rule supplies high-confidence future move labels and abstains on ambiguous cases. Its paired comparator is shift detection from consecutive generated states on the identical roots/actions. It measured the cue against state-pair matching under generated prefixes; the exact results appear below. No training or actor claim is attached.

## Completed frozen comparison and cost (2026-09-29 evening)

The planned same-weight comparison ran (`transport_equivalence.json`). Input-only stratification of the existing 1,002-root panel found 308 no-scroll, 592 scrolling/no-reentry, 102 scrolling/reentry six-frame windows. Fifteen from each group were read with identical frozen historical `corrt/fcanvas` weights; the original `tworld.py` source SHA was reconstructed exactly by reversing only the later `ldad1` pool-registry edit. New exact carry transport versus `fcanvas` had mean relative output RMSE **1.33e-7** with no scroll and **3.34e-7** with scroll/no reentry. On reentry it was **9.66e-4** (max absolute token-coordinate difference **0.1923**). Therefore a six-frame exact-carry retrain would largely duplicate the already run `fcanvas` mechanism. This is a frozen forward equivalence, not proof trained worlds converge identically, and reentry/off-screen memory remain distinct.

The first resource timing mixed JIT cold start with steady steps (`transport_resource_first.json`) and is retained as an invalid throughput comparison. The corrected warmed full optimizer-step test (`transport_resource_steady.json`, synthetic six-frame inputs, batch 40, three timed steps after warm-up) measures **0.7207 s/step, 2.281 GB peak allocated** for fixed-slot `fmamba`, versus **3.5459 s/step, 3.274 GB** for explicit carry transport: **4.92x** slower and **1.44x** peak allocated memory. It fits the 6 GB GPU, but is an expensive way to reproduce short-window `fcanvas`; prior matched historical `corrt/fcanvas` training took roughly 83 minutes versus `corrt/fmamba` 72 minutes for 6k updates.

The learned correspondence check also ran (`learned_gate_rollout.json`, per-root rows in `learned_gate_rollout_rows.pt`). A threshold chosen only on TRAIN-seed true one-step `corrt/fcanvas` logits was fixed at **-1.5254**. On **1,132** TEST true one-step move forks it achieved **0.9995 AUC** and **99.20%** moved/blocked accuracy. On TEST generated-prefix factual-action rollouts with independently calibrated visible-terrain labels, it beats frame-pair matching on **2,258** classifiable move steps: **78.65% versus 68.16%**, paired seed gain **+10.50 percentage points [7.19,13.86]**. At depth 9–16, **73.56% versus 57.62%**, gain **+15.93 points [10.80,20.92]**, but the learned gate's blocked-move specificity is only **45.19%** (AUC **0.713**, moved-action prior **70.46%**). The learned cue is more robust than matching two generated states, but remains unreliable exactly where a false carry shift would misroute history. The positive comparison does not establish that using it to move carries improves a world or actor.

**Decision:** Do not spend 6k–18k matched updates on the explicit bounded carry variant now. Its tested short-window mechanism already exists as `fcanvas`, with small/mostly unresolved predictive benefit, whereas the exact step implementation is 4.92x slower. Preserve it as a distinct long-context/reentry design candidate only if the ongoing main fmamba length/control results localize a deficit to cross-scroll carry identity. A future learned-gate version must use frame-outer/layer-inner scheduling (or an early cue), compare against a matched `corrt` no-transport control, and address generated-depth false shifts. Long-context actor gain remains unmeasured.
